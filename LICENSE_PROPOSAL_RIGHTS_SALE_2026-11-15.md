# Garden rights-sale proposal — ₹20 crore net to owner

**Status: PROPOSED / NOT ADOPTED / SUBJECT TO LEGAL REVIEW.** This document records a proposed route for a complete sale and transfer of Garden rights. It does not amend, replace, suspend, or override the current `LICENSE`. No rights are transferred by publishing this proposal, sending an email, or making a Git commit.

## 1. Commercial offer

The owner is willing to sell and permanently transfer all Garden rights that the owner legally owns and is entitled to transfer to the first qualifying purchaser who completes the requirements in Section 3.

**Minimum consideration:** the owner must receive **₹20,00,00,000 (twenty crore Indian rupees) net after all taxes payable by the owner in India in connection with the transaction**.

The intended economic result is that the owner retains ₹20 crore after applicable Indian taxes. Any withholding, deduction, transaction charge, or other amount that would reduce the owner's net receipt must be addressed in the transaction documents and funded by the purchaser in addition to the net amount, subject to applicable law and tax advice. The gross amount and tax treatment must be determined by qualified Indian tax advisers before signing.

## 2. Intended scope of transfer

Subject to ownership verification, third-party rights, applicable law, and a definitive written assignment, the intended transfer is a **full, exclusive, worldwide, perpetual and irrevocable transfer** to the purchaser of all transferable rights owned or controlled by the owner in Garden, including, where applicable:

- copyright and other intellectual-property rights in Garden source materials, specifications, documentation, designs, written works and other original project materials;
- transferable rights in names, marks, domains, repositories, project assets and associated goodwill, if owned by the owner;
- rights to use, reproduce, modify, commercialize, license, sublicense, distribute, publish, assign and further transfer the acquired rights;
- the right to control future licensing, governance, development and commercial exploitation of the acquired rights; and
- transferable rights in future Garden improvements created by the owner, only if expressly included in the definitive agreement.

The commercial intention is that the purchaser becomes the sole owner of the transferred Garden rights and the seller retains no continuing ownership or control over those transferred rights, except for any non-waivable rights or obligations under applicable law.

**Limits that must be respected:** the seller can transfer only rights actually owned or legally controlled by the seller. Third-party code, contributions, open-source components, data, patents, marks, repository-hosted material owned by others, and rights held by collaborators are not automatically included. A schedule must identify included assets, excluded assets, third-party dependencies, existing grants and any rights that cannot legally be assigned.

## 3. First-completed purchaser rule

The purchaser may be any eligible individual, company, institution, public body or other legal person, subject to identity, sanctions, anti-money-laundering, source-of-funds and other mandatory legal checks.

The intended priority rule is **first qualifying completion**, not merely first expression of interest. A purchaser qualifies only when both of the following have occurred:

1. The full amount required to deliver ₹20 crore net to the owner has been received as cleared, irrevocable funds in the owner's designated bank account; and
2. The purchaser's complete proposed definitive transfer agreement and required supporting legal documents have been received at the owner's designated email address, with the documents identifying the purchaser, the assets and rights to be transferred, the consideration, payment evidence, representations, warranties, closing conditions and transfer mechanics.

The order and time of receipt of both completed requirements must be recorded using reliable bank and email timestamps. The first purchaser to satisfy both requirements, and pass mandatory legal checks, receives priority for closing. A payment alone, an email alone, a draft agreement alone, or an unverified payment does not complete the requirements.

The owner must review and execute the definitive agreement. **No transfer is effective until the owner has accepted and signed the definitive agreement and all required closing steps have been completed.** If the first claimant fails legal checks, cannot establish cleared funds, or does not provide legally sufficient documents, the owner may reject that claim and consider the next qualifying claimant.

## 4. Confirmation and closing

The definitive agreement should specify the exact transfer date, asset schedules, payment and tax mechanics, representations and warranties, treatment of liabilities, third-party rights, repository and account handover, confidentiality, dispute resolution, governing law, and any mandatory filings or registrations.

After valid execution and completion, the owner will send a written confirmation email recording that the transfer has closed. The parties should also retain the signed agreement, proof of cleared funds, tax documentation and a closing inventory. Email confirmation is evidence of closing; it is not a substitute for any deed, registration, filing, consent or other formality required by law.

## 5. Review required before adoption or execution

Before this proposal is used as an offer or contract, independent Indian legal and tax professionals should verify:

- the seller's chain of title and authority to transfer each included asset;
- whether the proposed transfer is an assignment of copyright, patent, trademark, domain, repository, contractual or other rights, and the formalities required for each;
- the treatment of third-party contributions, open-source licenses, prior permissions, existing users and repository history;
- the correct gross consideration required to deliver ₹20 crore net after Indian taxes, including withholding, GST or other applicable transaction taxes if relevant;
- purchaser eligibility, source-of-funds checks, banking compliance, foreign purchaser/payment rules and any required approvals;
- enforceability of the first-completed purchaser mechanism and a fair process for timestamp disputes; and
- appropriate warranties, indemnities, liability allocation and dispute-resolution provisions.

This proposal is a statement of commercial intent for review. It is not a legal opinion, binding offer, executed assignment, tax calculation, or guarantee that every category of Garden material can be transferred.
