# Literal Multi-Agent Archive Audit — 2026-09-14

Status: **AUDIT / NON-CANONICAL / DOES NOT MODIFY GARDEN v15.5**

## Why this audit exists

The earlier `HISTORICAL_CHAT_UPGRADE_AUDIT_2026-09-14.md` explicitly used semantic retrieval and could not claim literal inspection of exported chats. This audit uses the user-supplied ChatGPT, DeepSeek and Gemini exports instead. It therefore serves as a validation/addendum, not a replacement for the earlier audit.

## Archive boundary and method

- 373 normalized conversations across ChatGPT, DeepSeek and Gemini.
- 25,737 normalized messages.
- Long-lived Gemini conversations were split by actual message time, because a conversation created in February could be reopened in August or September.
- Every normalized message was mechanically parsed and indexed.
- High-signal design sessions were then reviewed in bounded chronological passes rather than loading the full corpus into one model context.
- 90 exact long-payload hashes occurred across more than one agent (185 occurrences). Those copies were treated as lineage/review events, not independent invention or corroboration.
- Large user messages were often pasted AI artifacts. Short user prompts, corrections, accept/reject decisions and repeated requirements were weighted more strongly when inferring human intent.
- No private personal text is copied into this public audit.

## Main validation result

The previous historical audit is directionally strong. Its principal surviving candidates remain valid pending review: cross-scale causal discovery; behavioral/normative/mechanism drift; Human Assurance Reliability/HITL workload; UCAS/Kindergarten definition recovery; SCEP implementation lineage; justice-system scope; and outcome-based memory evaluation. The literal exports also confirm that many old named systems were evolutionary ancestors of current Garden rather than missing modules.

## Chronological architecture lineage recovered

### February — GAIA safety kernel and multi-agent refinement

The earliest available design lineage centers on GAIA: deterministic safety filtering, Byzantine/correlated-failure handling, explicit system modes, runtime contracts, audit certificates, physical envelopes and repeated adversarial iteration. Within days the user was manually feeding one model's output to another and asking them to cross-check each other.

Most of this is now subsumed by current Garden physical assurance, ActionGate/authority, degraded/emergency handling, proof/evidence and independent-review machinery. Two residual mechanisms were not explicit enough in current v15.5: hazard-monotonic action selection inside an already-authorized emergency envelope, and typed reuse of proven unsafe/infeasible planning regions.

### March–April — theories, falsifiability and persistent-agent control

The archive moves through TFT, VSM, FET and SRT, then toward self-regulating and persistent-agent architecture (APAO). The important engineering transition is from “write a theory” to “find a falsifier, identify the missing mechanism, add runtime state/verification, and ask another model to break it.” Current Garden already contains or explicitly tracks the surviving theory bindings and assurance structure; no duplicate top-level modules are proposed here.

### May — Digital Garden, branches, auditors, normative memory

May is the largest conceptual transition before formal GSL. Early Digital Garden discussions introduce recursive main/experimental branches, independent auditors, a public knowledge/service layer, normative memory, meta-governance, and repeated attacks on capture and hidden sovereignty. Several early normative ideas were crude or unsafe and are not carried forward (e.g. majority vote as truth, frozen universal cultural rules, or random authority as a generic tiebreak).

A useful residual is narrower: normative/legal precedent should be revalidated under material change rather than becoming self-sealing. This is preserved only as a refinement of the already-pending justice/drift workstream.

### June — reliability, routing and legal formalization

ReliabilityOS/ARP, multi-model routing, Themis and TRAIN emphasize planner/executor separation, evaluation-gated routing, evidence acquisition, observability, failover, physical/reliability constraints and formal legal reasoning. The earlier historical audit already correctly found most of this absorbed by current Garden; HITL workload/reliability remains the notable surviving gap.

### July — Garden ontology and GSL

The project changes character. Rather than accumulating standalone architectures, it starts defining a typed semantic substrate and claims/evidence/relations. The user increasingly asks whether old modules can be expressed through a common ontology instead of remaining separate. This is the key consolidation point that explains why many earlier names disappear without their useful mechanisms being lost.

### August–September — canonicalization and governance of the design itself

The archive shows rapid GSL/KR/AR/Garden versioning, then a move to immutable baselines, five-file canonical source, typed receipts, proof/evidence distinctions, policy/process algebra, per-step control, explicit provenance and repository-based multi-agent review. By September the process is less “invent a new theory” and more “compare against current owners, prove non-duplication, preserve lineage, test, and only then admit a delta.”

## Important NO_CHANGE conclusions

The exports do **not** justify new modules for copied-output independence, unknown-state handling, small trusted kernels, multi-agent review, general drift, cross-scale causality, continuous epistemic assimilation, memory compression, retrieval reuse, or model consolidation: those are already present or separately tracked.

The archive also strengthens the rejection of several early ideas as written: votes/repetition manufacturing truth, cultural taste frozen as constitutional truth, punitive preference becoming automatic justice authority, randomized authority as a universal governance primitive, and unsupported claims of certification or savings.
