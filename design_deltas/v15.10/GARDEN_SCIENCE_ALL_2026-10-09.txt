1.2 Projection-Loss Search
Detailed state X; coarse observable Y=A(X); dynamics T. Search for x1,x2 with
A(x1)=A(x2) but A(T(x1)) != A(T(x2)) in a declared domain. This demonstrates
that the projection is not closed for that prediction; it does not prove novelty.

1.3 Necessity-Challenge Search
Given a claimed prerequisite C for outcome Y >= y_min, seek an admissible
counterexample x with NOT C(x) and Y(x)>=y_min, with full mass/energy, cost,
safety and hidden-processing accounting. This tests necessity, not sufficiency.
TRIZ, constraint relaxation and scientific counterexample search are prior art.

1.4 Decision-Sufficiency Test (proposed Garden application of established
value-of-information theory)
A coarse projection Y is decision-sufficient for a stated context if no feasible
additional measurement Z increases expected net optimal decision value materially.
Define V(X,a) in dollars (or a separately typed objective), diagnostic cost C(Z):
Delta_net(Z) = E[max_a E[V(X,a)|Y,Z]]
             - E[max_a E[V(X,a)|Y]] - C(Z).
Under an exact common probabilistic model, the gross information value is
nonnegative, but real fitted predictors may have negative realized net value.
Two states with equal Y but different optimal actions indicate a potential
economic projection gap. Require a blinded held-out estimate of Delta_net,
uncertainty, intervention costs and strong modern comparators.
This is NOT a new mathematical theorem.

1.5 Scientific gates
G0 freeze contemporary baseline; G1 identify a measurable residual or costly
prerequisite; G2 typed equations and units; G3 conservation/identifiability;
G4 synthetic null recovery; G5 strong blind baseline comparison; G6 paper-level
prior art; G7 independent physical test; G8 measured incremental net economic
value; G9 independent admission. UNKNOWN never means PASS. The scientific
research register cannot modify Garden's canonical authority.

13.3 A1 GARDEN PHYSICAL AND ECONOMIC CONTRACT
Detailed state X: electrode inventories, loss of lithium inventory, active
material losses, transport/impedance distributions, thermal and usage history.
Coarse projection Y=(usable capacity Q [Ah], DC resistance R [ohm]).
Additional observations Z: partial dQ/dV or dV/dQ features (units explicitly
recorded per sensor protocol), optional EIS spectra, age/cycle history.
Action a: select application (low-rate storage, high-power service, recycle),
or select the next safe diagnostic measurement. Context: cell chemistry,
voltage window, temperature, regulatory and safety limits.
Outcome V(x,a) [USD/cell] is expected NET economic value, incorporating
revenue, remaining service life, failure/warranty risk, testing costs and
recycling value. Uncertainty must be estimated on held-out cells.
An economically sufficient coarse projection at tolerance epsilon [USD/cell]
would satisfy |V(x1,a)-V(x2,a)|<=epsilon for matched Y across a declared
state distribution. This is a research adequacy criterion, not an
assumption that conventional battery markets only use capacity.

Decision rule: a*(y,z)=argmax_a E[V(X,a)|Y=y,Z=z].
Baseline: a0*(y)=argmax_a E[V(X,a)|Y=y].
The Bayes optimal expected value of additional information Z is nonnegative
BEFORE test costs under an exact shared probability model. This is a known
decision-theoretic result, NOT a new physical law. Real fitted predictors
can overfit and make realized incremental value negative.
Primary estimand (USD/cell):
 Delta_net = E[V(X,a*(Y,Z))-V(X,a0*(Y))] - C_diagnostic.
For randomized deployment, also account for opportunity cost and pack
integration constraints. Value of a measurement is NOT the same as a
higher classification accuracy percentage.
Signed hypothesis to freeze: Delta_net > 0 for a declared population and
diagnostic cost threshold. Falsifier: held-out 95% confidence interval does
not support positive Delta_net after multiplicity and selection controls;
do not equate non-significance with proof of zero effect.
Potential ranking-reversal test: identify pairs matched on Q,R whose optimal
application ordering reverses when Z is known. Test whether the reversal
generalizes beyond training data and beyond the existing EIS model.

13.4 A1 EXPERIMENT AND SAFETY PROTOCOL
Pilot: approximately 20 retired cells, single chemistry and traceable source,
used ONLY to check feasibility, sensor repeatability and power/sample-size
estimation; cannot establish market-wide valuation gain.
Confirm capacity, DC resistance, EIS, partial voltage profiles at controlled
temperature and rest time; measure degradation-state proxies independently
where feasible. Randomly assign matched cells to two safe application-duty
protocols; a single cell cannot independently experience both lifetime
trajectories, so use matched randomized groups or validated counterfactual
methods with explicit assumptions. High-power cycling and thermal risk require
a qualified battery laboratory, containment, fire safety and authorization.
Blind the prediction team to eventual outcomes; freeze features and model
selection before lifetime follow-up. Strong comparators: EIS application
classifier, full ICA/DVA plus degradation model, and published economic
decision model. Include training, validation, and fully held-out batches,
chemistry/source transfer, calibration and bootstrap uncertainty.
Primary outcome: realized or properly censored net application value
(USD/cell or USD/kWh) after diagnostic costs. Secondary: lifetime
forecast error, Brier/calibration, misclassification, test minutes/cell.
An independent replication cohort is required before claiming economic value.
Timeline assumption: weeks for initial measurements; 3-9+ months for
accelerated pilot cycling; longer for representative field service life.
No cells purchased or physically tested in this work.

3.1 TEN WORKED MATHEMATICAL EXAMPLES

WORKED 002 — Battery electrochemistry: electrolyte concentration variance
Model/assumptions: For local electrolyte concentration c(x), let i(x)=i0 exp(-a/c(x)) for c>0, a>0 (illustrative transport-limited constitutive law, not universal Butler–Volmer). Aggregate I=E[i(c)].
Derived result: Taylor expansion gives I-i(E[c]) = (1/2)i''(cbar)Var(c)+O(E|c-cbar|^3), with i''(c)=i(c)*a*(a-2c)/c^4. Thus the leading correction is POSITIVE if cbar<a/2 and NEGATIVE if cbar>a/2, for sufficiently small variance.
Discriminating experiment: Two otherwise matched cells with the same mean c but different measured concentration variance should show the predicted sign change when a/cbar crosses 2. Reject if sign fails after independent measurement of a and confound controls.
Standing: derivation under stated illustrative assumptions; global novelty not established; no real-world test performed.

WORKED 006 — Battery degradation: local heat-current covariance
Model/assumptions: Assume local degradation r(x)=k exp[-E/(R T(x))] j(x)^p, T>0, j>0, k>0, E>0; compare E[r] with r(E[T],E[j]).

THEORY EQUATION CONTRACT (illustrative constitutive law, NOT universal SEI physics):
Let r(x,t)=k exp[-E/(R T(x,t))] (j(x,t)/j_ref)^p.
T [K]>0; j and j_ref [A/m^2]>0; E [J/mol]; R [J/(mol K)];
p dimensionless; k [damage-unit/time]. Thus r [damage-unit/time].
Spatial average D(t)=<r(x,t)>; lumped D0=r(Tbar,jbar).
Let a=E/R [K]. At small bounded fluctuations about Tbar,jbar:
D-D0 = 0.5*r_TT Var(T) + r_Tj Cov(T,j)
      + 0.5*r_jj Var(j) + third-order remainder.
r_TT=r*(a^2/T^4-2a/T^3) [damage/(time K^2)].
r_Tj=r*a*p/(T^2*j) [damage/(time K (A/m^2))].
r_jj=r*p*(p-1)/j^2 [damage/(time (A/m^2)^2)].
Each product with its variance/covariance has units [damage-unit/time].
For p>0, a>0, positive Cov(T,j) contributes positively to the SECOND-ORDER
correction, but the TOTAL correction can have either sign depending on
Var(T), Var(j), p, the domain and higher-order terms.
The constant-temperature/current limit recovers the lumped baseline exactly.
The approximation is not reliable for large gradients or near j=0.

11. NARROW NEW ENGINEERING CANDIDATE — ACTIVE FOULING IDENTIFICATION
Status: mathematical identification under assumptions; NOT a novel physical law.
Problem: infer a heat exchanger's flow-independent fouling resistance Rf while
convection changes with flow. Assume the simplified model
    R(q) = Rf + A*q^(-n),  q>0, n>0 fixed and independently calibrated.
Two stable flow points q1 != q2 imply
    Rf = [R(q1)*q2^(-n) - R(q2)*q1^(-n)] /
         [q2^(-n) - q1^(-n)].
Dimensions: R and Rf are thermal resistance [K/W] for a specified device,
A has [K/W] * [flow]^n. Formula is exact within the stated model, but
poorly conditioned if q1 and q2 are too close; flow perturbations can
change fouling or process state, invalidating the assumptions.
Research extension: choose safe perturbation pairs to minimize uncertainty
of Rf under a sensor-noise covariance model, while minimizing downtime and
process energy. Compare against published flow-compensated thermal/hydraulic
fouling diagnosis, adaptive observers and optimal experiment design.
Prior art exists for flow-compensated fouling estimates and experiment design;
worldwide novelty of this narrow combination is UNKNOWN.
Economic benefit if validated: lower unnecessary cleaning and recovered heat
transfer, conditional on avoided maintenance costs exceeding test disruption.
Falsifier: out-of-sample resistance estimates and maintenance decisions fail
to improve on a matched modern comparator.
Status: UNVERIFIED research candidate, no new empirical data.

#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""Reproducible candidate-026 synthetic closure experiment; no empirical/novelty claim."""
import json
import numpy as np
from scipy.linalg import expm

DT=0.25
TRAIN=320
TEST=160

def trajectory(seed, two_body, sigma=0.012):
    rng=np.random.default_rng(seed)
    n=TRAIN+TEST+2
    u=np.zeros(n)
    for t in range(n):
        u[t]=1.2*np.sin(0.073*t)+0.6*np.sin(0.019*t+0.7)+(0.8 if (t//29)%2 else -0.4)+0.2*rng.normal()
    if two_body:
        C1,C2,h,g=2.0,4.0,0.9,0.24
        # C1 dT1/dt = u - g*T1 - h*(T1-T2); C2 dT2/dt = h*(T1-T2)
        A=np.array([[-(g+h)/C1,h/C1],[h/C2,-h/C2]])
        B=np.array([[1/C1],[0.]])
        M=np.zeros((3,3));M[:2,:2]=A;M[:2,2:]=B
        E=expm(M*DT);Ad=E[:2,:2];Bd=E[:2,2]
        x=np.array([0.1,-0.6]); y=[]
        for k in range(n):
            y.append(x[0]);x=Ad@x+Bd*u[k]
    else:
        C,g=2.0,0.35
        a=np.exp(-g/C*DT);b=(1-a)/g
        x=0.1;y=[]
        for k in range(n):
            y.append(x);x=a*x+b*u[k]
    return np.asarray(y)+sigma*rng.normal(size=n),u

def matrices(y,u,p):
    # ARX(p,p) uses current and p-1 previous y and u to predict next sample
    X=[];z=[];ts=[]
    for t in range(p-1,len(y)-1):
        X.append([*([y[t-j] for j in range(p)]),*([u[t-j] for j in range(p)]),1.])
        z.append(y[t+1]);ts.append(t)
    return np.array(X),np.array(z),np.array(ts)

def evaluate(y,u,p):
    X,z,t=matrices(y,u,p)
    tr=t<TRAIN-1;te=t>=TRAIN
    # Fixed ridge is part of the preregistered model, not tuned on test.
    lam=1e-5
    L=np.eye(X.shape[1]);L[-1,-1]=0
    beta=np.linalg.solve(X[tr].T@X[tr]+lam*L,X[tr].T@z[tr])
    pred=X@beta
    train_mse=np.mean((z[tr]-pred[tr])**2)
    test_mse=np.mean((z[te]-pred[te])**2)
    # Training BIC with parameter penalty
    bic=tr.sum()*np.log(max(train_mse,1e-15))+X.shape[1]*np.log(tr.sum())
    return dict(train_mse=float(train_mse),test_mse=float(test_mse),bic=float(bic),coefficients=beta.tolist())

def run():
    # Calibrate the threshold on independent one-body null simulations; measurement noise can make ARX2 look better even when no hidden physical state exists.
    null_calibration=[]
    for seed in range(100):
        yc,uc=trajectory(seed+5000,False)
        bc=evaluate(yc,uc,1);ec=evaluate(yc,uc,2)
        null_calibration.append(1-ec['test_mse']/bc['test_mse'])
    null_threshold=float(np.quantile(null_calibration,0.95))
    allout={}
    for label,two in [('null_one_body',False),('hidden_two_body',True)]:
        entries=[]
        for seed in range(100):
            y,u=trajectory(seed+1000,two)
            b=evaluate(y,u,1);e=evaluate(y,u,2)
            improvement=1-e['test_mse']/b['test_mse']
            # require >= 10% test improvement AND BIC improvement of at least 10
            selected=improvement>null_threshold and e['bic']<=b['bic']-10
            entries.append({'seed':seed,'baseline_test_mse':b['test_mse'],'extension_test_mse':e['test_mse'],'fractional_improvement':improvement,'selected':selected,'delta_bic':e['bic']-b['bic']})
        allout[label]={'n':len(entries),'selection_rate':sum(x['selected'] for x in entries)/len(entries),'median_test_improvement':float(np.median([x['fractional_improvement'] for x in entries])),'median_baseline_mse':float(np.median([x['baseline_test_mse'] for x in entries])),'median_extension_mse':float(np.median([x['extension_test_mse'] for x in entries])),'cases':entries}
    allout['design']={'null_calibration_quantile':0.95,'null_calibration_threshold':null_threshold,'calibration_seeds':100,'dt':DT,'train_steps':TRAIN,'test_steps':TEST,'noise_sd':0.012,'seeds_per_condition':100,'comparison':'ARX(1,1) vs ARX(2,2) with equal train/test sequences','selection_rule':'heldout MSE improvement above independent null 95th percentile AND training BIC decreases by >=10','scope':'synthetic only; comparator is not strongest modern system identification; no novelty claim'}
    return allout
if __name__=='__main__':

def simulate(u, p, order):
    # Parameters positive, initial temperatures unrestricted; zero-order-held forcing.
    if order == 1:
        C,g=np.exp(p[:2]); x=p[2]; a=np.exp(-g/C*DT); b=-np.expm1(-g/C*DT)/g
        y=np.empty(len(u))
        for t,ut in enumerate(u):
            y[t]=x;x=a*x+b*ut
        return y
    C1,C2,h,g=np.exp(p[:4]); x=np.array(p[4:6],float)
    A=np.array([[-(g+h)/C1,h/C1],[h/C2,-h/C2]])
    B=np.array([[1/C1],[0.]])
    M=np.zeros((3,3));M[:2,:2]=A;M[:2,2:]=B
    E=expm(M*DT);ad=E[:2,:2];bd=E[:2,2]
    y=np.empty(len(u))
    for t,ut in enumerate(u):
        y[t]=x[0];x=ad@x+bd*ut
    return y

def fit(y,u,order):
    starts=([np.log(2),np.log(.35),float(y[0])],) if order==1 else ([np.log(2),np.log(4),np.log(.9),np.log(.24),float(y[0]),float(y[0])],)
    best=None
    for start in starts:
        start=np.array(start)
        nphys=2 if order==1 else 4
        lo=np.r_[np.full(nphys,-5.),np.full(order,-20.)]
        hi=np.r_[np.full(nphys,5.),np.full(order,20.)]
        result=least_squares(lambda p:simulate(u[:TRAIN],p,order)-y[:TRAIN],start,bounds=(lo,hi),max_nfev=250,ftol=1e-8,xtol=1e-8,gtol=1e-8)
        sse=float(np.sum(result.fun**2))
        if best is None or sse<best[0]:best=(sse,result.x,result.success)
    sse,p,success=best
    full=simulate(u,p,order)
    test_mse=float(np.mean((full[TRAIN:TRAIN+TEST]-y[TRAIN:TRAIN+TEST])**2))
    bic=TRAIN*np.log(max(sse/TRAIN,1e-15))+len(p)*np.log(TRAIN)
    return dict(bic=float(bic),test_mse=test_mse,fit_success=bool(success),params=p.tolist())

def run(n,noise,seed_start):
    out={'settings':{'n_per_class':n,'noise_sd':noise,'seed_start':seed_start,'selection_rule':'training BIC improvement >=10; output-error model, holdout reported independently','scope':'synthetic, structure-aware comparator; not blind discovery'}}
    for label,two in [('null_one_body',False),('hidden_two_body',True)]:        cases=[]
        for k in range(n):

            a=fit(y,u,1);b=fit(y,u,2)
            cases.append({'seed':k,'selected_two_body':b['bic']<=a['bic']-10,'delta_bic':b['bic']-a['bic'],'one_body_test_mse':a['test_mse'],'two_body_test_mse':b['test_mse'],'fit_success':a['fit_success'] and b['fit_success']})
        out[label]={'n':n,'selected':sum(c['selected_two_body'] for c in cases),'selection_rate':float(np.mean([c['selected_two_body'] for c in cases])),'median_one_body_test_mse':float(np.median([c['one_body_test_mse'] for c in cases])),'median_two_body_test_mse':float(np.median([c['two_body_test_mse'] for c in cases])),'cases':cases}
    return out
